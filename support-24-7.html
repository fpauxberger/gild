24/7 follow-the-sun support

Fictional service description for an imaginary company. Locations, times, staffing and service levels are invented examples.

1. What "follow the sun" means

GIT runs three regional support hubs, each staffed during its own daytime. As one region's working day ends, an open case is handed over to the next region, so every case is worked by a rested, daytime team and someone is always on shift, every hour of every day of the year. All hubs use one ticketing system, one knowledge base and one set of runbooks, so a customer never has to explain a problem twice.

Windows are defined in UTC; local clock times shift with daylight saving. Each hub also keeps a second engineer on call outside its shift as a safety net. Support language is English, with German, French and Spanish speakers on the relevant hubs.

3. The handover protocol

  1. 30-minute overlap: outgoing and incoming shifts work together before each handover.
  2. Structured handover record: for every open case, the engineer writes status, tests done, hypotheses, customer contacts, next action and deadline in a fixed template in the ticket.
  3. Live briefing for critical cases: priority 1 and 2 cases are briefed verbally on a bridge call, then the incoming engineer confirms ownership in the ticket.
  4. One owner at any time: every case has exactly one accountable engineer and one incident commander for critical events; the customer sees the name of the current owner.
  5. Continuous record: all actions, logs and decisions stay in the ticket, so nothing depends on anyone's memory.

7. Product-specific support

  • Trading and market products (GIT Terminal, GIT TradeHub): additional on-call specialists around market opening and closing in each region and during volatile periods.
  • Payments and banking (GIT PayFlow, GIT CoreBank): continuous monitoring of settlement cut-off times and instant-payment schemes; P1 support is always staffed, including weekends and bank holidays.
  • Risk, compliance and fund products (GIT RiskGuard, GIT Comply, GIT FundLedger): intensified coverage around end-of-day batches, reporting deadlines and NAV calculation.
  • Lending and wealth products (GIT LoanSuite, GIT WealthAdvisor): onboarding specialists and release-day support.

8. Security, data protection and regulation

  • Controlled access: engineers reach customer environments only through just-in-time, approved and recorded sessions with strong authentication; see Secure by design.
  • Data residency: customer data stays in the contracted region. Cross-region support work uses masked or synthetic data by default; access to personal data from outside the EU needs the customer's approval and a lawful transfer mechanism.
  • Regulatory incident support (DORA): for major incidents GIT delivers a classification assessment and an initial technical summary within two hours of detection, so customers can meet their own initial-notification deadlines, followed by intermediate and final root-cause reports.
  • Operational resilience: support tooling runs in multiple regions, and a documented continuity plan covers loss of a hub; remaining hubs take over its window.

9. How to reach support

Customer portal (24/7), regional phone hotlines routed automatically to the hub on shift, email, and a public status page with live service health. Critical issues should always be raised by phone to start the P1 process immediately.
