Secure by design: technical definition

Fictional reference architecture for an imaginary company. It describes design objectives and how they map to regulatory requirements; it is not a certification, audit result or legal advice. Regulatory status checked October 2026.

1. Definition

Secure by design means that security properties are specified as requirements before implementation, derived from a threat model, enforced by the architecture itself rather than added afterwards, and verified continuously across build, release and operation. Three rules follow: (a) secure by default: the shipped configuration is the most restrictive one and weakening it requires an explicit, logged, authorised change; (b) least privilege and default deny for every identity, process and network flow; (c) vendor accountability: GIT carries the security engineering burden and does not shift it to the customer through optional settings. The approach follows the Saltzer–Schroeder design principles, NIST SP 800-160 Vol. 1 (systems security engineering) and the joint CISA/NCSC "Secure by Design" principles.

2. The two claims on our start page, defined precisely

End-to-end encryptionSensitive payloads (account numbers, client identity data, order and position data, documents) are encrypted at the client or originating service with application-layer encryption (AES-256-GCM data keys, wrapped with per-tenant RSA-3072/ECDH P-384 key-encryption keys, JWE format). They stay ciphertext through every hop, queue and store, and are decrypted only inside the service component that must process them, inside an isolated runtime. Data at rest uses AES-256-XTS volume and AES-256-GCM field-level encryption.

4. How continuous compliance monitoring works

  1. Control catalogue: each control has an ID, a testable assertion, an owner, a frequency and mappings to DORA articles, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A and BSI C5 criteria.
  2. Automated tests: policy-as-code and agents collect configuration state and test it (for example "no storage without encryption", "no administrator without hardware MFA", "latest restore test younger than 90 days").
  3. Evidence store: results are signed, time-stamped and kept immutable so that auditors and supervisors can reproduce any past state.
  4. Key risk indicators: open critical vulnerabilities, mean time to patch, backup success rate, privileged-access exceptions, third-party SLA breaches. Thresholds trigger escalation to management.
  5. Review: results feed management-body reporting, the annual ICT risk review and lessons learned after incidents and tests.

5. Mapping to financial regulation

Role clarification. DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, applicable since 17 January 2025) places its obligations on financial entities. GIT acts as an ICT third-party service provider: it designs its products so that its customers can meet their DORA duties and agrees the contractual terms of Article 30. Responsibility for the customer's overall compliance stays with the customer (shared responsibility).

6. Limits and customer responsibilities

  • Customers decide classification of their own critical or important functions, their impact tolerances and their final register of information.
  • Customers configure their user roles, approval workflows and key-management option (GIT-managed or customer-managed).
  • Customer-side endpoints and integrations are outside the GIT control boundary unless covered by a managed service.
  • Specific targets (RPO, RTO, retention, patch times) are service-level values to be fixed in the customer contract.
